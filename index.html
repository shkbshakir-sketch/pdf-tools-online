After conversion, pick your download format — DOCX or PDF — in the format picker, then download in full original quality.

Good to know: PDF to Word creates a real, editable Word (.docx) file from your PDF — two-column pages are read in the right order (left column, then right), wrapped lines are rebuilt into whole paragraphs, headings keep their levels, and figures are extracted and placed back in the text. Best for text-based PDFs; scanned-image PDFs have no selectable text to extract. Extracted text is cleaned of the hidden control characters PDFs carry, so the DOCX opens in Word ready to edit — not view-only. If Word opens the DOCX in Reading View or with a yellow Protected View bar, click “Enable Editing” (on phone, tap the pencil/Edit icon) — Word asks this once for any downloaded file, then it edits normally. Word to PDF renders your .docx with its real layout — heading styles, bold & italic, alignment, paragraph spacing, lists, tables (as real tables) and images are carried into the PDF at the file's own page size, margins and column setup, so the PDF looks like the Word document, not plain text. Fonts are substituted with the closest built-in match (Helvetica / Times / Courier), headers & footers are not carried over, and special symbols and formula characters (Greek letters, subscripts, arrows) are shown as plain readable equivalents, so Word files of any size convert — small notes and book-length documents alike. Quality promise: outputs keep the original quality — images are embedded without re-compression and PDF pages render at high resolution (about 300 DPI) in every tool that turns pages into pictures (PDF to JPG, PDF to PowerPoint, OCR). The only tool that trades quality for size is Reduce File Size, and even there pages are rendered at 200 DPI or more — the saving comes from compression, never from shrinking the page. Big PDFs (100+ pages, 100 MB+) are processed page-by-page in chunks, so they finish without freezing — they just take a little longer on phones. For animated GIFs, Image to PDF uses the first frame.
